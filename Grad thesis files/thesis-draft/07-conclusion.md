# 7. Conclusion and Future Work

## 7.1 Summary of contributions

This thesis presented and characterized **DP-FedBN**, a dual-optimizer composition of DP-SGD with FedBN that preserves per-client BatchNorm specialization while applying differential privacy only to the parameters that actually cross the federation boundary. The composition recognizes that, under FedBN, the BatchNorm parameters and running statistics never leave the client and therefore require no DP protection in the standard federated threat model; only the convolutional and linear parameters that are transmitted need DP. A freeze-then-unfreeze maneuver makes Opacus accept the BatchNorm-containing model, and two parallel optimizers — one wrapped by the privacy engine for the non-BatchNorm parameters, one plain for the BatchNorm parameters — route each parameter group through exactly the update rule it needs.

The contribution is stated in deliberately measured terms. The name "DP-FedBN" already appears in ADCOL (ICML 2023) [1], where it denotes a one-paragraph, party-level Laplace baseline on aggregated model updates; this work claims neither that concept nor a new aggregation rule nor a new privacy mechanism. To our knowledge, it is the **first record-level DP-SGD composition of FedBN's BatchNorm-locality with explicit treatment of the Opacus engineering obstacle**, together with the first systematic medical empirical characterization of that composition across heterogeneity regimes and privacy budgets — two ECG tasks, six non-IID partition regimes, and three privacy budgets, totaling about forty configurations.

## 7.2 Principal findings

Three findings emerge from that characterization, each stated at the strength the evidence supports.

**First, the eval-regime flip that FedBN induces under label heterogeneity is task-agnostic.** FedBN underperforms in pooled-central evaluation while overperforming in per-client local evaluation, on both MIT-BIH five-class beats and PTB-XL binary records. On MIT-BIH label skew the central-versus-local gap reaches +0.72 macro F1; the same qualitative flip — FedBN below FedAvg on central, above on local — survives a complete change of task to PTB-XL. The broader lesson is that, in many realistic deployment scenarios, the choice between central and local evaluation matters more than the choice of aggregation method. It is a methodological variable that benchmarking studies, the original FedBN paper among them, have tended to leave implicit, and which can silently determine which method appears best.

**Second, DP-FedBN's local advantage under differential privacy is partition-dependent.** It preserves a +0.17 local-F1 edge over DP-FedAvg+GroupNorm on MIT-BIH label skew at $\varepsilon = 3$, where DP-FedBN wins; but it records −0.008 on MIT-BIH Dirichlet and −0.234 on PTB-XL Dirichlet at the same budget, where DP-FedAvg+GroupNorm wins. The mechanism is consistent in sign across both datasets: DP-driven activation noise propagates to each client's per-client BatchNorm parameters without the cross-client averaging that smooths it for shared parameters, so FedBN's locality — its strength without DP — becomes a liability under DP whose severity scales with how little class-truncation specialization there was to protect.

**Third, naive DP-FedAvg on a BatchNorm-containing model is empirically refused by Opacus's `ModuleValidator` at every finite $\varepsilon$.** This is not a side observation but the concrete motivation for the dual-optimizer formulation: a practitioner cannot simply attach DP-SGD to a BatchNorm-heavy network, and the two principled responses are either to replace BatchNorm with GroupNorm or to keep it local as DP-FedBN does.

## 7.3 Practical guidance

For medical federated-learning practitioners, the findings reduce to a short, heterogeneity-typed rubric. Use DP-FedBN when sites are label-truncated — specialty clinics and single-condition cohorts — and local deployment of a per-site model is the goal; there it preserves a clear local-F1 edge under DP, and the dual-optimizer overhead is justified. Use DP-FedAvg+GroupNorm when sites have proportional, mixed-but-complete class heterogeneity, where it is simpler and at least as good. And report **both** central and local F1 whenever the deployment regime is itself ambiguous between a single pooled model and per-site personalized models, because the two regimes can disagree on which method is best.

## 7.4 Limitations

The principal limitations bound the scope of these claims and motivate the future work below. All numbers are single-seed (seed 42), so seed variance is unquantified and the non-monotonicities observed across $\varepsilon$ should be read as single-seed signatures. A single fixed BatchNorm-heavy CNN1D architecture is used throughout. Heterogeneity is simulated by partitioning pooled datasets rather than measured across genuinely separate institutions, and the MIT-BIH central test set is an intra-patient stratified holdout. The federation has only five clients, where production systems have dozens to hundreds. FedProx is run at a single un-tuned proximal coefficient. The PTB-XL task is reduced to binary on a single lead. Validation is confined to ECG.

## 7.5 Future work

Several concrete directions follow directly from these limitations.

- **Multi-seed variance quantification.** This is the single most important deferred experiment. Repeating the full sweep across several seeds would put confidence intervals on every reported gap, confirm the robust findings (the eval-regime flip, the label-skew DP edge, the refusal of naive DP-FedAvg), and settle whether the non-monotonic $\varepsilon$ orderings on the Dirichlet partitions are real or single-seed artifacts.
- **Multi-label, twelve-lead PTB-XL.** The binary single-lead reduction was forced by the representational bottleneck of Lead II on the five-super-class task. Restoring all twelve leads and treating PTB-XL as a multi-label problem, with macro-AUC over the five super-classes as the metric, is the natural way to test whether the findings hold on a richer, clinically fuller task.
- **Architectural breadth.** Whether the eval-regime flip and the partition-dependent DP behavior persist under ResNet-style 1-D networks or Transformer encoders — architectures with different normalization footprints — would establish how much of the story is specific to a BatchNorm-heavy CNN.
- **Scale and realism of the federation.** Increasing the client count toward the dozens-to-hundreds of real deployments, and ideally validating on genuinely cross-institutional ECG data rather than simulated partitions, would test the findings under realistic covariate shift. Adopting a patient-disjoint, inter-patient evaluation split would further harden the central-evaluation numbers.
- **Stronger threat models.** The present threat model leaves the deployed personalized model's BatchNorm statistics unprotected, on the grounds that they never cross the federation boundary. A setting in which an adversary can issue repeated queries against a deployed per-client model could in principle infer those statistics, and extending DP-FedBN to protect the deployed model — not only the federated updates — is an open problem worth addressing before clinical deployment.
- **Tuning and method breadth.** A proper sweep of the FedProx coefficient, and the inclusion of the FedBN extensions deliberately set aside here (batch-specific normalization, personalized data transformations), would situate DP-FedBN within a fuller personalization landscape under DP.

Taken together, these directions would move DP-FedBN from a carefully characterized single-seed construction on two ECG tasks toward a validated tool for privacy-preserving, personalized medical federated learning. The construction itself — keep BatchNorm local, protect only what crosses the boundary, and report both evaluation regimes — is the contribution this thesis stands behind, in exactly those measured terms.
